A home in Redlands 360 comes with its own property tax layer. For 2026, the 2026 budget packet for Redlands 360 Metropolitan District No. 1 reports 15.000 mills for operations and 55.031 mills for debt service, a combined 70.031 mills on top of every other taxing entity that already covers the Redlands. The 2026 budget packet for District No. 2 reports 14.827 operating mills and 55.068 debt mills, for 69.895 total.

Those debt figures matter because the cap first published to buyers was lower. If you're weighing a new build off 23 Road against an established Redlands resale, the list price only gives you part of the comparison. The district levy has already gone past the cap printed in the sales materials, and it stays with the house for decades.

The cap on paper and the cap in practice

In January 2025, the developer's metro district brochure described "up to 50 mills" of debt levy for roads, sewer, water lines, storm drainage and public amenities. It said the levy would be "capped by City agreement and the District Service Plan." The districts' own disclosure page shows the same 50.000-mill maximum, followed by three words that do a lot of work: "subject to adjustment."

Item What the early materials said What the 2026 budgets certify
Operating levy, Canyon Rim 360 and Easter Hill 15 mills, "expected to remain consistent" 15.000 mills in District No. 1; 14.827 in District No. 2
Debt service levy Up to 50 mills, applied "as needed" 55.031 mills in District No. 1; 55.068 in District No. 2
Combined district levy About 65 mills at the stated maximum 70.031 and 69.895 mills
Total debt authorized Not listed in the brochure $60,000,000

The public pages don't explain how the adjustment works, and the service plan clause that defines it wasn't available for review. So the safe reading is narrow. The 50-mill figure is a starting point that can move, and in the first year the debt levy was actually collected, it moved up.

Why 2026 is the year the levy showed up

The brochure said the first debt issuance for the opening neighborhoods was expected "between 2025 and 2026." That's what happened. District No. 2's 2026 budget records limited tax general obligation bonds of $9.575 million and $4.302 million, issued December 9, 2025. The brochure also said this kind of debt is "typically amortized over 30-40 years."

So a buyer who toured Easter Hill in early 2025 was looking at a levy that hadn't been fully applied yet. A buyer touring today is looking at one that has. The 2026 property tax bills, which are based on 2025 assessed values, are the first that clearly show what owning in the district costs.

The statewide math was also shifting during this period. Colorado's Division of Property Taxation certified 2025 residential assessment rates of 6.25% for local governments and 7.05% for schools, "in place for 2025 only." The division's 2026 chart lists 6.8% for local government. A metro district levy is applied to assessed value, so changes in the assessment rate affect what a mill actually costs a homeowner. The two facts appear side by side here. No reviewed document says one caused the other.

A rough sense of scale

For a ballpark, take a home at the low $600,000s, where Aspen Leaf Innovations' Redlands 360 pricing currently starts. At a flat 6.25% local assessment rate with no reductions, $600,000 of value works out to about $37,500 in assessed value. Seventy district mills on that is roughly $2,600 a year. This is simple arithmetic, not a tax bill. State reductions, the specific parcel's tax area and every other overlapping levy will change the result. The Mesa County Assessor and the state's Colorado Property Tax Map are where you get the real number for a specific address.

Even with those caveats, the scale is clear. That's a yearly cost the district bill alone adds, and a nearby Redlands home outside a metro district doesn't carry it. A lower list price can be partly or fully offset by it over the life of the loan.

The second line item: special assessments

The mill levy isn't the only district obligation. District No. 2 also issued $7.385 million in Special Improvement District bonds in December 2025. These bonds are repaid differently. The budget describes them as payable "solely from and to the extent of" special assessments on property, along with any assessment lien sale proceeds. They're assumed to carry 6.25% interest, with mandatory principal payments starting December 1, 2030, and final maturity on December 1, 2045. The district budgeted $451,306 for SID bond interest in 2026 alone.

The public budget documents don't say which parcels are in the assessment district or how much each lot owes. That's the transaction detail to pin down. The way to find out is a title commitment and the county lien records for that specific parcel, before your inspection and objection deadlines pass.

What the tax layer pays for, and what the HOA still might

The district levy does cover real things. The developer's August 2026 FAQ says the mill levy pays in part for development, infrastructure and amenities. It adds that the trails and parks built this way are open "to the entire Grand Valley community and its visitors." The brochure presents the metro district as a way to keep HOA dues down, since the district maintains medians, open space and landscaping instead of the HOA.

There's still a separate Redlands 360 HOA. It's expected to cover resident-only amenities, which may include a community room, pool, fitness center and pickleball courts. None of the public materials reviewed give a dollar amount for those dues. Plan for both costs, and ask for the HOA budget in writing.

Redlands 360 is also paying for road work. The roughly $5 million roundabout at Broadway (Highway 340) and 23 Road started June 1, 2026, and is expected to be finished in December. Redlands 360 is responsible for construction and about 75% of the cost, and the City of Grand Junction covers the remaining 25% plus a future second lane. In the meantime, the city's October 5 traffic bulletin says Alcove Drive will stay closed at 23 Road for the rest of the project, with detours on Vista Grande Road or Columbine Drive. Highway 340 stays two-way at 35 mph through the work zone.

What each phase actually puts on the table

The district layer looks different depending on which part of Redlands 360 you're considering, because each phase has a different starting cost:

  • Canyon Rim 360: The first released neighborhood, unveiled in fall 2022 with 22 homesites and four preferred builders: Distinctive Design Build, Fixture Studio, Goetzmann Custom Homes and Maves Construction. The example lots currently listed are priced at $209,000, $219,000 and $229,000, before any construction.
  • Easter Hill: 51 single-family lots built by Goetzmann Custom Homes and Aspen Leaf Innovations. As of August, fewer than 10 lots remained and model homes were open. Aspen Leaf's pricing starts in the low $600,000s. Goetzmann's starts in the mid-$700,000s, and it has three move-in-ready spec homes.
  • 4H Peaks: 47 homesites, 11 from Goetzmann and 36 from Aspen Leaf, expected to release in late 2026 or early 2027. No pricing has been published yet.
  • The Corner @Redlands 360: 125 attached homes built by Lennar on 20.49 acres. Final approval came March 4, 2026. The plans run 1,099 to 1,664 square feet. The developer expects them in 2027. Local reporting in April 2025 had construction starting that year, so the timeline has already slipped once.

The lot buyer is in a different position from the others. A $219,000 Canyon Rim lot carries the district levy from the start, and your own construction timeline then adds to the assessed value that levy applies to. For anyone thinking about building here, the comparison is lot plus construction plus the district layer over the term of the debt. A finished resale elsewhere in the Redlands is the alternative to price against.

Redlands 360 is also still growing. City Council approved an amendment on August 5, 2026, that added about 29 acres and brought the plan to roughly 629 acres. At that point, four filings had been approved and three platted. The developer describes the approved plan at 1,300 to 1,750 homes. The brochure says growth inside the districts produces the tax revenue that pays down the debt. That means your yearly cost depends partly on how many more homes get built and how fast.

Before you write the offer

  1. Confirm which Redlands 360 Metropolitan District the parcel is in, and find its current certified levy on redlands360md.com.
  2. Ask the title company to show any Special Improvement District assessment or lien on that parcel.
  3. Get the full overlapping levy for the parcel's tax area from the Mesa County Assessor, and pull the same figure for any non-district Redlands home you're comparing it to.
  4. Get HOA dues and the HOA budget in writing, separate from the district taxes.
  5. On a lot purchase, estimate the district levy on the finished home's value, not just on the dirt.

A few straight answers

Will the debt levy go back down to 50 mills? The public documents don't say. The cap is published as "subject to adjustment," and the formula for that adjustment wasn't in the materials reviewed.

Does the operating levy change too? The brochure says the 15-mill operating levy is expected to stay consistent. District No. 2 certified 14.827 for 2026, so it's close to that figure but not identical across districts.

Who controls these decisions later? According to the brochure, the metro district will eventually be run by a board of residents.

Is this tax advice? No. It's a guide to the documents worth reading. A tax professional and the county records give the final figures.
