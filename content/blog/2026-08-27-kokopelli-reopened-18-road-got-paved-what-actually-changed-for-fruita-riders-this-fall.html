--- title: Fruita Mountain Bike Trails This Fall: Key Updates for Riders date: 2026-08-27 slug: kokopelli-reopened-18-road-got-paved-what-actually-changed-for-fruita-riders-this-fall description: Fruita mountain bike trails this fall have changed fast. See what reopened at Kokopelli and what's new at 18 Road before your next ride out. ---

A text thread among a few regulars at the 18 Road trailhead this month went something like this: "Trails are open again." Followed by, "Which ones?" That second question turned out to matter more than it should have. Fruita's two flagship trail systems spent 2026 on completely different tracks, and riders who assume they're back to normal in the same way are going to be surprised either way they guess.

Kokopelli just came back from a wildfire closure that shut down the entire McInnis Canyons National Conservation Area for weeks. 18 Road never closed at all. Instead it spent the year with a paved access road in place while crews finished off the last stretch of a trail expansion that's been under construction since 2022. Two systems, two entirely different 2026s, and if you haven't ridden either one since spring, neither will feel quite like you remember.

The fire that closed half the county's public land

The Snyder Fire started near Glade Park in late June and grew fast enough that Governor Polis declared a state of emergency and authorized the National Guard within a day of the initial closure order. The Bureau of Land Management's Grand Junction Field Office sealed off the entire McInnis Canyons NCA, home to the Kokopelli Loop Trails, along with a wide stretch of BLM land north of DS Road and west of 16 1/2 Road. That's not a narrow trail closure. That's most of the public land west of town.

The reopening happened in stages. Access to the Ruby Horsethief section of the Colorado River came back in early July as suppression efforts stabilized. The rest of the closure, including the Kokopelli system itself, stayed in place until the BLM rescinded the final emergency closure order on August 7, 2026, reopening every previously closed acre in Mesa County and neighboring Grand County, Utah.

That's a little over two weeks before this post, which means if the last time you rode Mary's Loop or Horsethief Bench was in June, you're overdue and the terrain has changed. The BLM's own language on the reopening is direct: fire-affected landscapes remain dynamic as vegetation, soils, roads and trails recover, and conditions can shift without warning. A burned area emergency response team has already assessed the ground and mapped out stabilization work that's expected to continue for another year. None of that means the trails aren't rideable. It means the exposed, sun-baked sections that used to have juniper cover in spots might not anymore, and loose soil on slopes that lost their root structure can behave differently after rain than it did last season.

18 Road's other kind of overhaul

While Kokopelli was closed to the west, 18 Road spent the year getting worked on for a completely unrelated reason: it was too popular for its own infrastructure. The Bureau of Land Management logged roughly 107,000 visitors to the North Fruita Desert during the 2024 fiscal year, and the gravel access road hadn't kept pace. Mesa County, the BLM, and the Federal Highway Administration finished paving about six miles of 18 Road from the Government Highline Canal to the campground, a project that wrapped in December 2025. County Commissioner Cody Davis, who's ridden that stretch for over a decade, told the Grand Junction Sentinel: "You can get out there a little quicker now, and a little safer." The road used to turn to a mess in wet weather. Now it's a 32-foot paved surface with dedicated shoulders for bikes, pedestrians, and horses.

The paving is the more visible change, but it's really the final piece of a bigger project that's been running since 2018, when Fruita, the BLM, Mesa County, and the Colorado Plateau Mountain Bike Trail Association first started planning a 32-mile trail expansion to relieve pressure on a system that was getting crowded past its design. Construction on new singletrack began in 2022, and by mid-2025 crews had built somewhere between 25 and 28 of those 32 planned miles, according to Mesa County's trails program supervisor. The last stretch, a technical segment running along the base of the Book Cliffs, needed extra survey work and heavier equipment because of the terrain.

That final trail has a name now. It's called K2-Play, and it's being built in honor of Kristina Kittleson, a name the local riding community will recognize even if you're new to the area. COPMOBA has organized volunteer work days through 2026 specifically to finish it, writing in its own event listing that "we can't wait to get it done!"

Where to actually ride this fall

Fall is the season both systems are built for. The high, exposed trails at 18 Road like Sarlacc, Edge Loop, Skywalker, Cicada, and Cloud City carry a seasonal closure from December 1 through May 1. Right now, in late August moving into September, they're wide open and this is close to peak condition.

A few things worth knowing before you load the bikes:

  • 18 Road's flow trails (Zippity Do Da, PBR, Kessel Run, Chutes and Ladders, Joe's Ridge, Prime Cut) are the reliable, dries-fast option and now carry legal e-bike access for Class 1 pedal-assist bikes, a change from a few years back.
  • 18 Road's soil is bentonite clay, which means a rainy week can shut things down for days even after the sky clears. If it's rained recently, give it time before you head out.
  • Kokopelli's classic loops (Rustler's Loop, Mary's Loop, Steve's Trail, Horsethief Bench, Wrangler's) reopened August 7 along with everything else, but ride with more attention than usual on sections near the fire perimeter. Loose or unstable ground in recently burned terrain isn't always obvious until you're on it.
  • Kokopelli's harder lines (Hawkeye, Mack Ridge, Troy Built, and the double-black Moore Fun) are worth a second look before committing, since these tend to be less traveled and less monitored for post-fire hazards than the main loops.

If you're not sure what a given trail looks like this week, Over The Edge downtown and Colorado Backcountry Biker on South Park Square both stay current on conditions and are worth a call before a longer drive out.

One date worth blocking off

The Hotdogger lands on September 26, 2026, at the 18 Road Trailhead, running sunrise to sunset. It's part endurance mountain bike race, part hot dog eating contest, and entirely a fundraiser for COPMOBA, the nonprofit that's spent years organizing the volunteer labor behind both the North Fruita Desert expansion and ongoing Kokopelli trail work. Registration opened back in June, and given how much attention both trail systems have gotten this year, this is likely to be a bigger showing than usual. It's also a good excuse to see the K2-Play progress in person if the finishing touches are done by then.

For a post-ride debrief, Hot Tomato Cafe and Copper Club Brewing remain the two anchors most locals cycle between depending on the crowd, and Aspen Street Coffee Company off the circle downtown is still the standard pre-ride stop for anyone who needs caffeine before the drive out to either trailhead.

The takeaway for anyone who rides here regularly

The version of Fruita's trail network you knew in spring isn't quite the one sitting out there right now. Kokopelli spent about six weeks closed and reopened just over two weeks ago with terrain that's still settling. 18 Road spent the same stretch of the calendar working on more trail mileage and attaching a name to the system's last unfinished piece, with its smoother road already in place. Neither change is finished settling in, and both are worth seeing for yourself before the weather turns.

That kind of week-to-week texture, knowing which road just got paved, which trail just got a name, which closure just lifted, is the same texture that matters when you're trying to understand a neighborhood as a place to actually live in it. The Griffin Team spends as much time on Fruita's trails as its listings, and if you're weighing a move here because of exactly this kind of outdoor access, that's a conversation worth having before you start looking at houses.
