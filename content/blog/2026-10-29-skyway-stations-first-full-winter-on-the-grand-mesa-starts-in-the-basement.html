Grand Mesa Nordic Council's new building at Skyway is in its first full ski season this winter. Most of the attention will go to the warming room with the wood stove. The bigger change is downstairs, where the snowcat that grooms the trails now spends the night in a sheltered bay with solar power to keep its battery and engine warm.

That matters more than it sounds. Last winter the Mesa's grooming fleet ran on backup equipment most of the time. GMNC built Skyway Station mainly so the snowcat can do more of the work.

What the building does before anyone arrives

Construction on Skyway Station started in summer 2025 and finished in April 2026. GMNC says the building will open for use at the start of the 2026–27 ski season. The lower level is set into the hillside and out of sight. GMNC calls it a sled shed, with permanent storage for snowmobiles and grooming equipment and a seasonal shelter for the snowcat.

GMNC explains the reason plainly. At 10,000 feet, cold mornings are common, and the snowcat is hard to start on them. With the solar system warming the battery and engine, the council expects fewer delayed starts and a longer life for the machine. The equipment has already moved in. At the May board meeting, operations manager Tigran reported that the snowmobiles and the snowcat were inside Skyway Station.

Last winter, by the machine that groomed it

The numbers from last season show what the Mesa's trails are working from. Tigran reported these figures for "this year" in GMNC's May 2026 board minutes:

Season reported in May 2026 Days
Total season length 147
Days groomed by snowmobile 83
Days groomed by snowcat 35

The minutes say the low snow forced the snowmobile grooming. In a thin year, a snowmobile packs and grooms where the heavier snowcat can't work yet. GMNC's 2023 master plan describes that same setup: snowmobiles handle early-season and low-snow grooming, and the snowcat takes over at Skyway and County Line once there's enough base. Ward is groomed by snowmobile.

The building helps on the mornings when the snow is already there. When the base is deep enough and the temperature is far below freezing, the snowcat should start on time. That means the wide, snowcat-groomed corridors GMNC advertises on its event pages.

GMNC hasn't announced a date to start grooming this season. The grooming report and the weather and road conditions page are the places to watch once snow falls. That page also notes that the COtrip camera on Highway 65 at Skyway was being replaced, with no date given for when it will be back.

The room upstairs is where the season gathers

The ground floor is the part skiers will use. GMNC describes it as a heated community space with a wood stove, and solar power keeps a minimum temperature and runs the lights. It's meant as a place to change clothes, refuel and warm up. It's also the meeting point for lessons, kids' programs, races and social events.

The first event built around the building is the 2026 Grand Mesa Nordic Festival and Masters Ski Weekend, Thanksgiving weekend at Skyway Trailhead. Classic clinics run from 10 to 11:30 a.m. and skate clinics from 1 to 2:30 p.m. on Friday and Saturday. On Sunday, both clinics run in the morning. There's a 3 p.m. social at Skyway Station on Friday, November 27. On Saturday, November 28, a 3 p.m. wax clinic starts with a talk inside and moves outside for the demonstration, because waxing isn't allowed inside the building. Clinics cost $65 for one session, $120 for two, $170 for three, $220 for four and $250 for five.

Who pays for the snowcat's garage

Skyway Station was a $1.6 million project. KKCO 11 News reported in January 2026 that GMNC had reached its initial fundraising goal. The final bill came in higher. GMNC's July 15, 2026 update said code requirements added $35,000 in unexpected costs, about two percent of the total budget, and that $25,000 still had to be raised. The goal was to close that gap by the start of the season.

The summer money also went into finishing work around the building. Volunteers helped complete the landscaping and the reclamation the Forest Service required. Crews reworked the drainage after watching how snow slid off the roof and melted last winter. They also added a gradual gravel ramp to the front porch and lined the front of the parking lot with boulders so cars can't drive up to the building.

The trails run on the same donation model. GMNC suggests a $20 trail-use donation per person, per day, for one-time visitors. The board's May 2026 minutes explain why it's a request and not a charge. GMNC operates under a Forest Service Special Use Permit, which covers the trails, Skyway Station and events. The permit lets GMNC ask for donations but not charge fees, including fees to rent Skyway Station.

If you ski the Mesa more than a handful of times, GMNC's 2026–2027 membership form lists these levels:

  • Limited income: $40
  • Individual: $100
  • Family: $175
  • Friend: $250
  • Super Friend: $350
  • Snow Angels Club, Strider: $500
  • Snow Angels Club, Trail Breaker: $1,000

GMNC says gifts to the building qualify under its Enterprise Zone designation through the end of 2029. They're eligible for a 25 percent Colorado state tax credit that can be used over five years. That comes from GMNC's own description of the program. This post is general information and not tax advice, so check how the credit applies to you with your tax professional before you count on it.

Where the dog goes

Skyway Station sits on the dog-free side of the Mesa's Nordic trails. Under GMNC's posted dog rules, the whole County Line and Ward trail systems are dog-friendly. Skyway is dog-free by consensus of the ski community. The snowmobile corridor on Scales Lake Road and Kannah Crossing marks the boundary, and signs are posted there. According to those rules, the Forest Service requires dogs on a 6-foot leash in parking lots and at trailheads. On the trails, dogs have to be physically restrained or in sight and under voice control. There's no trash service at the trailheads, so bagged waste has to be packed out. County Line has small shovels on the numbered sign posts for scooping waste off the trail.

So the warm new building and a skijoring morning happen at different trailheads. If you're out with a dog, plan on County Line or Ward. Women's Wednesday is the one recurring program set there.

What could change around the building, and what hasn't been decided

GMNC's 2026 board minutes list several projects that are still in progress:

  • Moving the warming hut. GMNC is working with the Forest Service to move its canvas warming hut from Skyway to County Line, and it's seeking a grant for a $4,000 replacement canvas skin. No completion date has been given.
  • New trails. The Forest Service wants some proposed sections to wait until logging projects near Skyway and County Line are finished. In July the board said a few trails away from the logging might be buildable this year and left it "to be determined."
  • A third groomer. The FY2027 budget includes a third groomer and more hours for the operations manager and lead groomer. A purchase hasn't been announced.
  • Snow removal at the building. In June the board discussed buying a stand-behind mini-loader to clear snow around Skyway Station, at a researched price of $25,000 to $50,000. The minutes don't record an approved purchase.

Every item on that list involves grooming equipment or the space around it, the same work the building was designed to support. The address for all of it is 3019 Hwy 65, Mesa.
