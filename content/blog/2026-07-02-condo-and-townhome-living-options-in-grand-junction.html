If you want a home that cuts down on yard work without giving up location, style, or access to Grand Junction’s daily amenities, condos and townhomes deserve a close look. In a market where single-family homes still make up most of the housing stock, attached homes can offer a practical path for first-time buyers, downsizers, relocators, and anyone who wants a simpler routine. This guide breaks down where to look, what price ranges to expect, and what to watch before you buy in Grand Junction. Let’s dive in.

Why attached homes matter in Grand Junction

Condos and townhomes are a smaller part of the local market, but they play an important role in Grand Junction’s housing mix. City planning documents note that Grand Junction will need about 11,400 additional housing units by 2040, and the current housing inventory is still mostly single-family homes.

That matters if you are looking for more choice. As the city grows, attached homes are part of the bigger conversation around housing options, convenience, and price points that fit different stages of life.

What the market looks like now

Grand Junction’s attached-home market is active, even if it is not huge. Recent market snapshots show about 35 condos for sale with a median listing price of $215,000 and about 71 townhouses for sale with a median listing price of $390,000. Similar live listing counts from Zillow show 39 condos and 77 townhomes.

Mesa County’s MLS-based update puts the year-to-date median sold price for townhouse and condo properties at $331,250 through February 2026. For context, Redfin reported the broader Grand Junction housing market median sale price at $418,784 over the three months ending April 2026. In simple terms, attached homes can offer a lower entry point than the overall market, depending on the property type and location.

Where condos and townhomes cluster

City planning points more housing variety and higher-density development toward places like Downtown, the University District, transit corridors, and other intensification areas. If you are searching for condos or townhomes in Grand Junction, that helps explain why certain pockets show up again and again.

Downtown and Dos Rios

Downtown is one of the clearest places to find attached-home options. Current listings and city records point to homes on College Place, Garden Grove Court, Bookcliff Avenue, Colorado Avenue, and near the 28 1/2 Road corridor.

Dos Rios adds another layer to the downtown story. This 58-acre mixed-use riverfront project includes park space, the Riverfront Trail, and direct access to downtown. The city has recorded Crawford Row Townhomes at Dos Rios Filing 2, and McWilliams Ballard describes Crawford Row as 56 newly built townhomes with 3 to 4 bedrooms, private outdoor space, and 2-car garages.

Downtown planning also supports a variety of housing choices at multiple price points, along with stronger pedestrian and bike connections to nearby amenities. In 2025, the Downtown Development Authority discussed a 24-unit townhome concept aimed at singles and two-person households, with pricing in the high $300,000s.

North Grand Junction and Horizon Drive

North Grand Junction is another major attached-home cluster, especially around Horizon Drive and the 81505 to 81506 area. Listings in this part of town often appear in communities like The Glen at Horizon Drive, Ptarmigan Point, Fountain Greens, Summer Hill, Thunder Mountain, and Fountainhead.

Many homes here are marketed around convenience. Listing details often mention cul-de-sacs, detached garages, front-yard maintenance, common-area care, and quick access to shopping, restaurants, Canyon View Park, and Patterson Road. If easy day-to-day logistics matter to you, this area is worth a look.

Redlands, The Ridges, and Redlands Mesa

On the southwest side of Grand Junction, attached homes tend to move more upscale. Redfin listing activity points to The Ridges, Redlands Mesa, and the broader Redlands area as some of the more active places for higher-end condos and townhomes.

These homes often lean into the outdoor lifestyle that draws many buyers to the Grand Valley. Listing features commonly mention Lunch Loop trails, Redlands Mesa Golf Course, views toward Colorado National Monument, rooftop decks, and lock-and-leave living. If you want lower maintenance without stepping away from a premium setting, this segment stands out.

Orchard Mesa and 81504

Orchard Mesa and nearby 81504 pockets offer a wider value range. Current attached-home listings in this area run from the mid-$200,000s to the mid-$300,000s, which can make it appealing for buyers who want a more approachable entry point.

This area also includes newer low-maintenance options. BOA Builders’ Meridian Park is a 31-townhome Orchard Mesa project designed for lock-and-leave living, with homes starting at $395,000. According to the builder, the HOA covers snow removal, trash, fiber internet, and common-area maintenance.

Typical price ranges to expect

Grand Junction attached homes generally fall into three practical price bands. Knowing those bands can help you narrow your search faster and avoid comparing very different types of properties.

Under $250,000

This is where you will often find smaller condos and older attached units. Current examples in the market have included homes like 950 Northern Way #23, 566 1/2 Garfield Dr. Unit C, 2150 College Pl #21, and 555 28 1/2 Rd #17.

If your top priority is affordability, this range can be a useful starting point. You may need to stay flexible on size, finishes, or age of the property.

About $250,000 to $450,000

This is the core move-up and low-maintenance range for many buyers in Grand Junction. It includes a large share of the local 2- to 3-bedroom condos and townhomes, and the countywide median sold price of $331,250 sits right in the middle of this band.

In practical terms, this range often offers the best mix of space, location, and lower-maintenance living. It can work well if you want a primary residence that feels manageable without giving up too much square footage.

Mid-$500,000s and up

Premium attached homes generally start in the mid-$500,000s and can climb into the $700,000s and $800,000s, especially in The Ridges and Redlands Mesa. Recent examples have included 362 Cliff View Dr, 2688 Summer Hill Ct, 386 W Ridges Blvd #A, and 390 W Ridges Blvd Unit A.

This tier often reflects view corridors, newer construction, upgraded finishes, and outdoor-lifestyle access. It can be a strong fit if you want lock-and-leave convenience in a higher-end setting.

Who condo and townhome living fits best

Attached homes are not one-size-fits-all, but they do line up well with a few common buyer goals. In Grand Junction, they tend to fit people who value convenience more than yard work.

You may want to focus on condos or townhomes if you are:

  • Downsizing and want fewer exterior chores
  • Relocating and need an easy home base while you learn the area
  • A busy professional who wants simpler upkeep
  • Looking for a lock-and-leave setup for travel or weekend time away
  • Shopping for a lower-maintenance option at a range of price points

Location can shape the fit too. Downtown and Dos Rios may appeal if you want trail access and a more connected in-town feel. Horizon Drive may suit buyers who prioritize shopping, services, and easy errands. Redlands and The Ridges often attract buyers who want trails, golf, and views. Orchard Mesa can make sense if you are looking for value or newer low-maintenance construction.

What to watch before you buy

The biggest thing to remember is that not all attached homes work the same way. The HOA structure can make a major difference in both your monthly costs and your day-to-day experience.

Some active listings say the HOA covers exterior maintenance, landscaping, water, sewer, and trash. Others include front-yard care or common-area maintenance. In Meridian Park, the HOA also includes snow removal and fiber internet.

That is why you should read the HOA details carefully before making a decision. Two homes at a similar price can feel very different once you compare what the monthly dues actually cover.

It also helps to keep Grand Junction’s overall layout in mind. Even if you buy a low-maintenance home in a convenient area, the city is still considered minimally walkable, with a Walk Score of 32. For many errands, you should still expect to drive.

How to shop smarter in this segment

Because attached-home inventory is smaller than the single-family market, the best opportunities can move quickly. A clear plan can help you compare homes with less stress.

Start with these questions:

  • What matters more to you: lower price, newer construction, or location?
  • Do you want a condo, or do you prefer the feel of a townhome with more separation?
  • How much HOA coverage would actually make your life easier?
  • Will you need garage space, storage, or room for outdoor gear?
  • Do you want quick access to downtown, shopping corridors, trails, or golf?

This is also where local guidance matters. In a market with options spread across Downtown, Horizon Drive, Redlands, and Orchard Mesa, a home’s value often comes down to the mix of maintenance, location, and lifestyle fit rather than just square footage alone.

If you are comparing newer townhomes, renovation potential, or different build qualities, having a broker who understands construction details can be especially helpful. Small differences in layout, materials, and HOA structure can affect long-term comfort and resale value.
