At the top of Powderhorn's west side, a drive terminal now sits where no lift has had its motor before. A 100-ton crane set it there piece by piece. That included the engine that will pull the new Wild West Express, which was serviced in Grand Junction by Leitner-Poma of America before it went up the Mesa. Further down the hill, crews built tower foundations on ground too steep for a concrete truck. A helicopter flew the concrete in one bucket at a time, more than 150 cubic yards in a single day, which the crew called a record.

Powderhorn lists Friday, November 20 as its tentative opening day for the 2026-27 season. The whole mountain is expected later. If the plan holds, the winter Grand Valley skiers think of as Powderhorn starts in mid-December, once the new lift on the west side starts running.

November opens the east side first

In August, KKCO 11 News reported that Powderhorn plans to open the east side around Thanksgiving and the west side in mid-December, depending on weather. That's two openings, about three weeks apart, in the same season.

The resort hasn't said which lifts will run on opening day. Its hours page names Easy Rider, Discovery and Explorer as lifts scheduled to reopen this winter. It doesn't say which of them will be spinning on November 20. The resort calls the opening date tentative, so the first weekend depends on snow, cold and how much terrain is ready.

What's waiting on the west side

Powderhorn has 54 trails, and 19 of them can only be reached by the lift on the west side, according to KKCO. Until the Wild West Express opens, more than a third of the trail count is out of reach by chair, even on a good snow day.

For years, the West End double served that terrain. Powderhorn spokesperson Ryan Robinson told KKCO the old lift had loyal longtime riders, but people also skipped it at times because of long waits and no footrests. The new lift runs on the same alignment, so no terrain access is lost, and the ride up the west side gets shorter. Robinson described the jump in capacity to KKCO:

"We are moving from what's called a fixed grip double chair lift, two people per chair at a consistent speed, to a high-speed quad lift. So faster access up the mountain and higher capacity, four people on a chair versus two."

The trip covers about 7,000 feet. Robinson called the west-side terrain "underutilized and maybe even a little underappreciated," and said better access should spread skiers across more of the mountain. In its July construction update, Snowbrains noted that with the Flat Top Flyer and the Wild West Express both running, high-speed lifts will serve nearly every skiable acre at Powderhorn. No new trails come with it. Pacific Group Resorts' announcement described the project as better access to existing terrain.

This matters most for skiers who have been heading to the Flat Top Flyer by default. The west side is where those 19 trails are, and this is the first winter a fast chair has served them.

Where the build stands in mid-October

Powderhorn has posted progress all summer in a series it calls Wild West Weekly. In order, the log shows:

  1. Bottom return terminal set. Cranes lifted the newly lettered return terminal onto its foundation. The motor won't live at the base, because the Wild West Express is a top-drive lift. The old West End had its motor at the bottom.
  2. Haul rope delivered. The rope arrived weighing 65,000 pounds. It still has to be spliced into one continuous loop from the bottom terminal to the top and back.
  3. Tower foundations on steep ground. A spider excavator dug the footers where regular equipment couldn't reach, and helicopters flew in the forms and then the concrete.
  4. Chairs and grips refurbished. Crews tore down, cleaned and inspected every grip. The tower sheave assemblies, the wheels that guide the haul rope, were rebuilt for an updated safety monitoring system.
  5. Drive terminal at the top. The 100-ton crane placed the drive terminal and its engine.
  6. Tower fly week. The latest entry, posted around Color Weekend and before Oktoberfest, said the helicopter was coming back that week to fly the tower assemblies onto their foundations one at a time.

No public update so far confirms the towers are all standing, the rope is spliced or the chairs are hung. The same entry noted that fall rain gave crews muddy, difficult conditions for heavy equipment, and that they used every work window they could get. Pacific Group Resorts' original announcement targets public opening for December 2026. For now, December is a target, not a confirmed date.

Last winter shows why December matters

The 2025-26 season shows how loose a Grand Mesa calendar can be. Powderhorn planned to open November 19, 2025, but a stretch of warm weather left runs with neither natural nor machine-made snow, the Daily Sentinel reported. The resort finally opened on Friday, December 5, after nearly a foot of snow and colder weather, according to a follow-up Sentinel story.

The end of the season came early too. KREX reported that Powderhorn closed on Monday, March 23, 2026, after 108 days, citing inconsistent snowfall and warm temperatures. Coverage said the resort had cut back operations as dirt showed through. The resort's homepage still promises more than 250 inches of snow in a typical year across more than 1,600 acres. Last winter fell short of that.

Put the two seasons side by side. Last year's real opening day was December 5. This year, the resort's 60th Anniversary Birthday Party falls on December 5 and 6, 2026. The ribbon cutting for the Wild West Express is on the calendar for December 18.

The dates to save, in order

  • Saturday, October 17, 6 to 8 p.m. Mountain Dinner Series at Powderhorn.
  • Friday, November 20. Tentative opening day.
  • Around Thanksgiving. East side planned to open, weather permitting.
  • Saturday, December 5, 9 a.m. through Sunday, December 6, 4 p.m. Powderhorn's 60th Anniversary Birthday Party. The event page doesn't list details yet.
  • Mid-December. West side planned to open with the Wild West Express, weather permitting.
  • Friday, December 18. Ribbon cutting for the Wild West Express.

The resort hasn't said whether the ribbon cutting is also the first day of public rides. If you want to be on one of the first chairs, keep an eye on the Wild West Weekly page through November.

A few smaller things on the hill this winter

One chair may stand out. Snowbrains reported that Powderhorn has hinted at a special commemoration for Chair No. 60 on the Wild West Express to mark the anniversary. The resort hasn't said what that will look like.

The base lodge has also been rearranged since the old West End made its last run. In September 2025, Pacific Group Resorts announced it would move the rental shop to the lodge's southwest side and open a new Quickdraw Café at the northeast corner. The same plan included more parking and a new grooming snowcat. The resort's hours page shows Quickdraw Café operating in summer 2026.

On passes, the resort said in its Wild West Weekly log that season pass prices would go up September 7. Check the resort's ticket shop for the current price. The capped three-year pass announced in February at an introductory $999 may or may not still be on sale.

The summer bike park closed for the season on October 4. Between now and November 20, the only thing changing on the hill is the new lift going up the west side.
