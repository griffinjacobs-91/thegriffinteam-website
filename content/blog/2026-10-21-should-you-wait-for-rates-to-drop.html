The short answer

For most Grand Valley buyers, waiting for rates to fall is a gamble with poor odds right now. The major forecasters expect 30-year rates to stay above 7% through 2027, while local inventory is the highest it has been since 2014. That combination means more choice and more negotiating room today, and no clear payoff for sitting on the sidelines.

That said, the right answer depends on your timeline, your savings and your job. Below, I walk through where rates are headed, what our local market is doing, and the actual dollars at stake, so you can make the call for your own situation.

I'm Kelley Griffin, a Realtor with Coldwell Banker Distinctive Properties and The Griffin Team, helping buyers and sellers across Fruita, Grand Junction, the Redlands and the Grand Valley.

Where mortgage rates are, and where they're expected to go

Rates have moved up this fall, not down. Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed was 7.28% on October 1 and 7.40% on October 8, compared with 6.34% a year earlier. The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate in mid-September, and its next meeting is October 27–28.

The forecasts that lenders watch have shifted the same way:

  • Fannie Mae (October 2026): about 7.4% for the rest of 2026 and 7.5% in every quarter of 2027. In September it had expected 6.7% for next year.
  • Mortgage Bankers Association (October 2026): above 7% through 2027, easing only to about 7.1% by late next year.

Forecasts are not guarantees, and they have been wrong in both directions. But when the two biggest forecasters both revise up, "rates will be lower by spring" is a hope, not a plan.

Active listings were spread across the valley: 173 in North Grand Junction, 136 in the Redlands and 133 in Fruita. In August, homes took a median of 65 days to sell, up from 59 a year earlier, and supply reached 4.4 months, the most since early 2017.

Two things stand out. First, prices haven't dropped. They've held roughly flat, so waiting for a "crash" isn't supported by the local numbers. Second, sellers are negotiating. In Grand Junction, more than half of recent sales (57.7%) closed under list price, per Zillow.

Conditions vary by price range. Homes under $500,000 still move faster, while higher price ranges have more supply and more room to negotiate.

Now weigh that against what waiting can cost you:

  • Rates could rise instead. Fannie Mae's current forecast is 7.5% for 2027, slightly above today.
  • Prices could tick up. If the same home costs 3% more next year, that alone adds about $77 a month at today's rate.
  • Your negotiating edge could shrink. If rates do fall meaningfully, buyers who have been waiting will return at once, and today's inventory and price cuts can disappear quickly.
  • Rent keeps going out the door every month you wait.

The saying "marry the house, date the rate" holds up here. You can refinance later if rates drop, though refinancing has its own costs and no one can promise when, or if, it will make sense. You can't go back and renegotiate the purchase price.

Ways to soften today's rate

In a market with this much inventory, you have tools that buyers didn't have a few years ago:

  1. Ask the seller to pay for a rate buydown. With a 2-1 buydown on the example loan above, the rate drops 2% in year one and 1% in year two, saving about $488 a month, then $249 a month. That costs roughly $8,800 up front, which a motivated seller may cover as a concession instead of a price cut.
  2. Ask for seller-paid closing costs. Keeping cash in your pocket can matter as much as a lower price.
  3. Look at new construction incentives. Builders often offer rate buydowns or closing cost help to keep homes moving.
  4. Talk to your lender about loan options. An adjustable-rate mortgage, a different down payment, or buying points yourself may change the math. A local lender can run side-by-side comparisons for you.
  5. Negotiate on price. With homes sitting longer, an offer below asking is often a reasonable starting point, depending on the home and how long it has been listed.

Which of these makes sense depends on the specific home and seller, and that's where a local agent earns their keep.

When waiting does make sense

Buying now isn't right for everyone. Waiting can be the smart move if:

  • You might move again within a few years. Closing costs on both ends can outweigh the benefits of owning for a short stay.
  • The payment would stretch you thin. If the monthly number only works with no cushion for repairs or emergencies, it's worth building savings first.
  • Your job or income is uncertain. Stability matters more than timing the market.
  • Your credit is close to a higher tier. A few months spent improving your score can earn a better rate than waiting for the market to move.

The key is that these are reasons about your situation, not predictions about rates. If you're ready on those fronts, today's market gives you room to buy on good terms.

Frequently asked questions

Will mortgage rates go down in 2027? The major forecasters don't expect much relief. As of October 2026, Fannie Mae projects about 7.5% through 2027 and the Mortgage Bankers Association expects rates to stay above 7%.

Are home prices dropping in Grand Junction? Not meaningfully. The Mesa County median sale price was $414,000 in September 2026, up 1.4% from a year earlier. What has changed is the number of homes for sale and how long they take to sell.

Is it a buyer's market in Mesa County? It's shifting that way. Inventory is the highest since 2014, and more than half of recent Grand Junction sales closed under list price. Homes under $500,000 remain more competitive than higher price ranges.

Can I get the seller to pay for a lower rate? Often, yes. Seller-paid buydowns and closing cost credits are common requests in this market. Whether a seller agrees depends on the home and how long it has been listed.

Should I buy now and refinance later? It can work, but only if the payment is comfortable at today's rate. Treat a future refinance as a possible bonus, not something your budget depends on.

What's the first step if I'm thinking about buying? Talk to a local lender to get pre-approved and see your real numbers, then sit down with a local agent to look at what's available in your price range and preferred areas.
