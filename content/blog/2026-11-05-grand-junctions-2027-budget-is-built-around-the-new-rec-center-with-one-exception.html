--- title: Grand Junction's 2027 Budget Is Built Around the New Rec Center, With One Exception date: 2026-11-05 slug: grand-junctions-2027-budget-is-built-around-the-new-rec-center-with-one-exception description: Finishing the rec center cuts Grand Junction's 2027 capital spending 38%, while the pool, splash pad and City Hall Fridays face trims. Council votes Nov. 18. ---

"Sometimes decisions that make sense on paper don't fully reflect the impact they have on people," Grand Junction City Manager Mike Bennett said on Oct. 21. He said it in the City's announcement dropping a plan to move Senior Recreation Center activities into the new Community Recreation Center. The Senior Center will stay at 550 Ouray Ave.

That paper decision didn't come from nowhere. Much of the proposed 2027 city budget leans on the CRC at 2836 Recreation Way. Finishing the building is a main reason capital spending drops. Its strong first weeks were part of the case for consolidating. And most of the proposed trims fall on the smaller, older city places around town. The Senior Center is where that consolidation hit its limit.

Six weeks in, the building carrying the weight

The CRC opened for regular operations on Sept. 28. KREX/WesternSlopeNow reported opening-day yoga and residents gathering after more than two years of construction. Voters approved the project in 2023, and ground broke on June 1, 2024.

The building is big. According to the City's grand opening announcement, it has five pools with two water slides, a pool climbing wall, a lazy river and a diving board. It also has an indoor climbing wall, a gym, fitness and group exercise spaces, an outdoor fitness area, a synthetic turf field, a game area, Kids Club and community rooms.

Demand showed up early. In late July, about two months before opening, KKCO 11 News reported about 1,500 annual memberships sold, against a City target of 7,900. Older residents signed up especially fast. At the Oct. 5 budget workshop, Bennett said about 1,700 people had signed up for the CRC's free SilverSneakers program in the building's first week, KJCT reported.

That 1,700 figure matters for the rest of this story. It's the number behind the idea that one new building could handle work spread across several older ones.

Why a finished building shows up as a smaller budget

The City's Oct. 5 recommended budget presentation put the 2027 total at $317.4 million, less than 1% above 2026. Capital spending fell 38%, which the City credits to finishing the Community Rec Center and to lower transportation capacity spending. Even with that drop, the overall budget barely moves, because the money is moving into labor and away from construction.

The pressure comes from operating costs. According to Western Slope Trellis's Oct. 10 civic wrap-up, City revenue has grown 30% since 2021 while expenses grew 53%. City finances went from an $11.3 million surplus in 2021 to a structural gap of about $6 million. The draft cuts 13 positions, 10 of them vacant, for about $2 million in savings, and still adds six police officers and three fire battalion chiefs.

By Oct. 26, the updated recommendation had grown to $318.8 million. Most of the increase came from spending shifted from 2026 into 2027. It includes $3.1 million in ongoing General Fund reductions. The plan also draws $2.3 million from the General Fund balance. The presentation says plainly that this buys time but can be spent only once.

So the new building is open and paid for, and the rest of the system is being trimmed to cover ongoing costs. That's where the everyday changes come from.

The proposed trims, place by place

Here are the resident-facing changes from the Oct. 5 presentation, with the detail staff gave at the workshop. All of them are still proposals.

Place or service What's proposed What it means on a weekday
City Hall Customer service windows closed on Fridays Staff keep working, taking calls, online requests and appointments
Lincoln Park Pool About an hour and a half less per day, Monday through Thursday Shorter weekday sessions at the older pool
Dos Rios splash pad Reduced staff coverage Less staffing at the riverside play area
Ash tree program Ends The City program stops
Printed City calendar Ends after the 2027 edition Saves about $60,000 a year
Typical utility bill About $9.74 more per month Applies to a household using 3,000 gallons of water, with single-family sewer and a 64-gallon trash cart

The City says specific hours and implementation dates are still being finalized. On the utility side, most residential trash rates stay the same, and curbside recycling stays free.

Lincoln Park Pool loses weekday hours the same year the city opens a building with five pools. The Dos Rios splash pad loses staff coverage the same year the CRC adds a lazy river and water slides. None of these trims closes a place outright. They shave hours and staffing at older sites while the new one runs at full schedule. The Friday City Hall change is similar. Trellis reported that staff said keeping the windows open would mean bringing back administrative positions that were cut.

The one move that didn't hold

The Senior Center was the boldest version of that idea. At the Oct. 5 workshop, Bennett described it this way, according to KJCT:

"One of the transitions in 2027 with the opening just last week of the Community Recreation Center is shifting the Senior Center to the Community Recreation Center in our recommended budget."

KJCT also reported that Councilmember Scott Beilfuss raised concerns. He noted the Senior Center building had recently been renovated and serves many seniors on low and fixed incomes. Trellis reported that Beilfuss suggested delaying the move a year if possible. Councilmember Anna Stout argued that the council has to make fiscal decisions for the whole community. Two days later, during public comment, a retiree who uses the center asked the council to consider alternatives, such as leasing space from the county.

On Oct. 21, the City changed course. The recommended budget will be revised to cover about $100,000 a year in facility and utility costs for the Senior Recreation Center in 2027. The City and the nonprofit that runs the center will work out a new agreement by January 2027, with a focus on the facility's long-term sustainability.

That leaves older residents with two places in 2027. The CRC is where those 1,700 SilverSneakers sign-ups landed. The center on Ouray Avenue is the one the community asked the City to keep. The SilverSneakers numbers showed the new building could draw seniors. The reversal showed that drawing them is a different thing from replacing a place they already use.

What the budget adds while it trims

The trims are only part of the 2027 plan. The Oct. 26 update describes a proposed $93.5 million capital program. Close to $50 million goes to sewer projects, including Phase II of the Persigo Wastewater Treatment Plant expansion. There's also $8.2 million for water infrastructure, including Juniata Reservoir improvements.

Here's what you'll see around town:

  • River Trail: a proposed $3.7 million expansion along C ½ Road to fill a gap in the trail network. The Oct. 5 presentation describes it as more than 80% grant funded.
  • Roads: improvements to B ½ Road, 26 ½ Road and North Avenue, plus about $3.5 million for street maintenance
  • A new signal: a traffic light at 7th Street and Elm Avenue near Grand Junction High School and Colorado Mesa University

The trail item stands out. Outside dollars cover most of it, so recreation spending keeps growing in a year when city-funded recreation hours are being shaved.

The dates that settle it

  1. Nov. 4: Council's first public presentation and hearing on the budget.
  2. Nov. 18: Second reading and public hearing. Council is scheduled to consider adoption that night.
  3. Jan. 1: The 2027 budget year begins.
  4. By January 2027: The new Senior Recreation Center agreement is due.

Until Nov. 18, every row in that table is still a recommendation. The full budget presentation and supporting documents are posted on the City's website.
